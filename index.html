Heritage & lime mortar

Old buildings need the old ways.

Lime mortar is made mainly of lime, water and an aggregate such as sand. It was the original material for stone buildings, long before Portland cement arrived in the 19th century.

Its soft, porous properties suit softer materials like natural stone and terracotta. That's why, for the repair of older stone-built structures and the restoration of historic buildings, cement has largely been ruled out, and lime is the right choice.

FAQ

Good questions before you start.

What kind of plastering do you do?
Internal and external plastering for renovations, new builds and commercial projects, including skim and hardwall finishes, metal stud ceilings and partitions, insulated dry-lining, external wall insulation, self-coloured renders (Monocouche, Weber, K-Rend, Parex), spray plaster, dashing, decorative cornice and lime mortar work.
Do you work with lime mortar on old stone buildings?
Yes. Lime mortar plastering is used for conserving buildings originally built with lime, repairing older stone-built structures and restoring historic buildings, inside and out.
I've just got planning. When should I ask for a plastering price?
